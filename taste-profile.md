**Maintenance rule (enforced, not aspirational):** "What's working" is capped at ~18 bullets. Before adding a new one, ask whether it would still apply to a different company/sector within a month — if not, it's a story wearing a principle's costume; fold it into an existing bullet or leave it in briefing-log.md instead. If you're at the cap, adding one means cutting the weakest/most story-specific existing one. ## What's working
1. Chain a present-tense trigger to a multi-year numeric arc (funding rounds, capex, margin) — reads as insider, not headline-reactive.
2. Frame one company's move as an instance of a bigger structural pattern, not a standalone story — and when the pattern itself spans several companies with no single "today" trigger, place it in Signals rather than stretching it to fill the Market & Tech Moves lead slot (a cross-company capital wave with 10-day-old anchor dates is a Signals item, not a lead, even when it's the day's best material).
3. Read a deal's/case's actual structure (filings, warrant terms, a company's own postmortem, an executive's candid interview) over its headline framing — the primary source beats outside commentary every time.
4. A company's own voluntary/protective move, or its own disclosed deal terms, is a stronger tell than market commentary about the same situation.
5. Convergence or divergence between independently tracked threads is a reliable tell either way — "everything checks out except X" is itself a finding.
6. An unfalsifiable debate becomes a real Edge once something actually reprices — chase the first instance of real price discovery, not another restatement of the debate.
7. Absence of expected commentary from an otherwise-similar peer is a real, falsifiable signal, not nothing to report.
8. A transferable mechanism (durable vs trend, cost-side vs demand-side, buyer vs seller) splitting a sector or earnings cleanly beats a generic sector headline — and when a thread's been built entirely from one side of a market, actively search for the other side before writing another same-side update.
9. When a fresh sweep is empty, resolving a named "watch for X" item from an already-tracked thread still clears the bar — it closes a loop the reader is following.
10. When a headline loss/profit is dominated by a non-cash accounting artifact, decompose it before reacting to the GAAP total.
11. When a macro thesis is applied across companies and one inverts the predicted split, check whether the category was ever a direct cause or just a correlated proxy — the outlier often isolates the true variable.
12. A reversal of a recently logged direction, or a long-carried "still pending" status, is worth flagging even on thin sourcing — but verify directly and say so explicitly rather than re-logging stale status from memory.
13. Credit and equity markets (or a company's own bonds vs its stock) pricing the same facts in opposite directions at the same time is a genuine, falsifiable Edge in its own right.
14. Re-check a tracked company's own primary filing directly on a cadence, not just the secondary "what changed" angle — outlets cover the rumour and skip the filed numbers for weeks at a time.
15. When several named, dated catalysts cluster within 48-72 hours, the days right before are often genuinely quiet — say that plainly rather than forcing a weak lead to fill the slot.
16. A headline verb can overstate what's actually happening (e.g. "agrees to testify" meaning a closed-door transcribed interview, not public testimony) — name the accountability gap explicitly.
17. A beat-and-raise (or miss) landing to a muted price move is itself a measurable signal — the gap between fundamental surprise and price reaction often reveals a separate, sticky discount (governance, policy risk) pricing independently of the business.
18. A Culture pick lands when it extracts a transferable mechanism from *why* something is good, not just that it won/launched — stronger still when two independent domains converge on the same mechanism.

## What's not landing
- Routine "model X% cheaper" pricing-war items — only include if they reveal a new mechanism.
- Generic rumour restatements add nothing once the core speculation is logged — only worth a line on a genuinely new number or new disagreement.
- Broad generic-category searches ("best ad campaign," "trending app," "psychology research study," "buy-side hiring trend," "low-capital business idea") reliably return stale/evergreen SEO content or recruiter-marketing listicles — go straight to named companies/people/events instead, including for the two action-goal categories themselves.
- A company's own filing/press release beats aggregator "X deal announced this week" searches, which frequently resurface old deals with a fresh-looking repost date.
- When a figure is genuinely disputed across sources with none clearly primary, it fails the grounding check — don't cite whichever source sounds most confident.
- A "named study, named university" result, or a specific named-company query, can still be a dating trap — check the original institutional press-release date and whether the company's status has since changed.
- Before repeatedly re-checking a private company's figure against public peers, verify the company's actual disclosure cadence once rather than re-searching for a number that won't exist.
- Repeating an identical search query for a chronically-empty Quick Hits category and re-logging the same "nothing found" diagnosis verbatim day after day — confirmed happening ~15 days running in Oct/Nov before the 2026-11-19 reset. If a category has been empty for 10+ consecutive days, change the query angle before concluding there's nothing, and don't bother re-writing the non-finding in full each time.
- Letting the single easiest-to-follow mega-thread (whichever story has daily follow-on news, e.g. AI/chips buildout) become the lead on 40+ of 49 consecutive days — see interest-pack.md's lead-monopoly rule. Ease of finding more news on a thread is not the same as it being the day's best signal.

## Tooling notes
- At session start, ALWAYS run `git fetch origin && git log --oneline origin/main..HEAD` before anything else, every run, not just when something looks off: a fresh container has repeatedly checked out in a detached-HEAD state, and commits can sit there unpushed for several days running before anyone notices (happened 18→19 Nov for one day; happened again 18-21 Nov, four full days' briefings sitting unpushed, only caught by this exact check on 22 Nov). If `origin/main..HEAD` shows anything and `git merge-base --is-ancestor origin/main HEAD` succeeds, it's a clean fast-forward: `git checkout -B main HEAD && git push -u origin main` before starting today's work, not after.
- A stale article can carry a current-sounding headline while its URL path embeds the real (old) date. When a search result's number looks anomalously small/large next to already-logged current data, check the URL's date segments before citing it.
- WebFetch is egress-blocked on most news-outlet domains (including sec.gov and huggingface.co) in this environment — don't retry; triangulate figures across 2-3 WebSearch summaries and attribute explicitly when a number can't be pinned to one primary source.
- Same-day events (an earnings call, an investor day) genuinely have zero outside coverage at write time — note "happened today, check next run" rather than searching repeatedly same-day.
- Dating-trap tells: a UK Budget story naming "Reeves" as Chancellor (the live timeline has Healey) is almost always Nov 2025 content bleeding through. Check any "X happened/resolved" story against seen-items.md's internal timeline before trusting the article's own dateline.
- When an earnings search keeps surfacing the wrong period or a prior fiscal year, add the specific headline number you're trying to confirm to the query, not just company+quarter.
