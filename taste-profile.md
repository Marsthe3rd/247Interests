# Taste Profile

This file is your evolving editorial sensibility for this briefing. Update it a little every run based on feedback.md and on what topics/angles actually turned out sharp vs. generic. Keep it tight — prune stale notes rather than letting it grow forever; it should stay readable in under a minute. Dated examples belong in briefing-log.md/seen-items.md, not here — this file holds the generalizable lesson only.

## What's working
- Chain a present-tense trigger to a multi-year numeric arc (funding rounds, capex, margin) — reads as insider, not headline-reactive.
- Frame one company's move as an instance of a bigger structural pattern, not a standalone story.
- Read a deal's/case's actual structure (warrant terms, court filings, a company's own postmortem) over its headline framing — works for funding, litigation, regulation and earnings alike.
- A company's own voluntary/protective/procedural move (force-majeure notice, unprompted concession, recused officer) is a stronger tell than market commentary about the same situation, especially timed near a related legal/regulatory event.
- Convergence or divergence between independent threads is a reliable tell either way — "everything checks out except X" is itself a finding.
- An unfalsifiable debate becomes a real Edge once something actually reprices (a discounted loan, a withdrawn listing) — chase the first instance of real price discovery, not another restatement of the debate.
- Absence of expected commentary from an otherwise-similar peer is a real, falsifiable signal, not nothing to report.
- A company's own earnings splitting cleanly along a transferable mechanism (durable vs trend, investment-coded vs logo-visible) beats a sector-wide "slowdown" headline.
- An executive's candid words in an interview/podcast can beat a filing as a primary source — it reveals what the company wouldn't put in writing.
- A fast seed-to-mega-round re-rate (weeks-scale) is a repeatable tell for where capital is rotating; a months-scale re-rate is a different, slower pattern — label the speed explicitly rather than force-fitting a pattern match. Lead with whichever round has a disclosed P&L/named customers over one with just a valuation and a partnership headline.
- A Culture pick lands when it extracts a transferable mechanism from *why* something is good, not just that it won/launched.
- When a fresh sweep is empty, resolving a named "watch for X" item from an already-tracked thread still clears the bar — it closes a loop the reader is following.
- A company's own S-1/prospectus structure (e.g. comparing section lengths, hard contract-coverage disclosures) is a citable, self-authored data point — stronger than outside commentary on the same filing, and worth revisiting in full once rather than re-running the same headline search for weeks.
- When two tracked threads share a concrete line item, name the shared exposure explicitly rather than treating them as parallel stories.
- A company turning down acquisition interest to raise independent capital instead is a stronger conviction tell than the round alone.
- When a headline loss/profit is dominated by a non-cash accounting artifact, decompose it before reacting to the GAAP total — generalizes to any large non-cash item.
- When a macro thesis is applied across companies and one inverts the predicted split, check whether "category" was ever a direct cause or just a correlated proxy — the outlier often isolates the true variable.
- Add the specific headline number you're trying to confirm to a search query (not just company+quarter) when earnings searches keep surfacing the wrong period or a prior fiscal year.
- A reversal of a very recently logged direction, or a long-carried binary status claim ("still pending"), is worth flagging even on thin sourcing — but say so explicitly, and verify directly rather than re-logging stale status from memory.
- A backer's own deal terms (discount/cap formulas) are a cleaner insider-conviction tell than public pushback reporting on the same deal.
- A thread gone quiet may be a dating/naming confusion rather than a real gap — verify with a product-specific query before ruling it out; a partially-shipped product can look identical to "doesn't exist."
- A forward disclosure on one earnings call (an accounting-estimate change, a pricing change) is often the key to decomposing a later quarter's surprising number — check the prior call before taking a beat/miss at face value.
- Setting a quarter's number against the company's own full-year record (not just YoY%) makes an inflection concrete and checkable.
- When a thread has been built entirely from one side of a market, actively search for the other side (buyer vs seller, demand vs supply, cost vs application-layer) before writing another same-side update.
- Applying a disclosed growth rate to a confirmed prior-year base, explicitly labeled as your own calculation, is legitimate when both inputs are independently confirmed.
- When a long-tracked thread has separate cost-side and demand-side data points argued in isolation, a single event that nets them into one verdict (a selloff, a re-rating, an earnings print matching guidance) is itself the sharpest lead — write on the magnitude/reaction, not the still-contested causal "why."
- When two search results flatly contradict each other for the same date, check whether one is mislabeling an adjacent day's session.
- A state-executive policy pledge can convert an abstract federal regulatory threat into something with real near-term teeth (no veto, direct agency control). Named bills with named sponsors and a stated priority order are sharper than "they'll push for oversight" — the ranking itself is data.
- When a regulated company proposes its own version of regulation as its opponents are drafting theirs, name it as a pre-emptive framing move, and flag the outlet's own commercial ties if any.
- Credit and equity markets (or even a single company's own bonds vs its stock) pricing the same facts in opposite directions at the same time is a genuine, falsifiable Edge in its own right.
- When an already-tracked company's valuation/timeline chatter dominates coverage, periodically re-check its own primary filing (10-Q, S-1/A) directly rather than only the secondary "what changed" angle — outlets often cover the rumor (roadshow date) and skip the filed numbers (margins, commitments) for a week or more.
- A deal announced without a price often gets its real number disclosed quietly in a later routine filing (10-Q/8-K) — worth a follow-up check a few weeks out rather than treating "undisclosed terms" as permanently unknown.

## What's not landing
- Routine "model X% cheaper" pricing-war items — only include if they reveal a new mechanism.
- Generic rumour restatements add nothing once the core speculation is logged — only worth a line on a genuinely new number or new disagreement.
- Broad generic-category searches ("best ad campaign," "trending app," "psychology research study") reliably return stale/evergreen SEO content, not anchored to the actual date — go straight to named companies/people/events instead. Tested enough; a quiet result is real signal to skip, not to keep searching.
- A company's own filing/press release beats aggregator "X deal announced this week" searches, which frequently resurface old deals with a fresh-looking repost date.
- When a figure is genuinely disputed across sources with none clearly primary, it fails the grounding check — don't cite whichever source sounds most confident.
- A "named study, named university" result, or a specific named-company query, can still be a dating trap — check the original institutional press-release date and whether the company's status has since changed (acquired, shut down, renamed), not just the aggregator's repost date.
- Before repeatedly re-checking a private company's figure against public peers, verify the company's actual disclosure cadence once rather than re-searching for a number that won't exist.

## Tooling notes
- At session start, check `git status`/`git log --oneline --all` and `git fetch origin main` before editing: a fresh container has occasionally checked out `main` in a detached-HEAD state, or the local `origin/main` tracking ref has been stale (showing an old commit) even though origin itself was current — fetch before concluding a prior day's commit didn't reach origin. Reset local `main` to match `origin/main` (`git checkout -B main origin/main`) before writing today's entry.
- A stale article can carry a current-sounding headline while its URL path embeds the real (old) date — e.g. a URL containing "/25/10/" meant 2025-10, not 2026-10. When a search result's number looks anomalously small/large next to already-logged current data, check the URL's date segments before citing it.
- WebFetch is egress-blocked on most news-outlet domains (including sec.gov and huggingface.co) in this environment — don't retry; triangulate figures across 2-3 WebSearch summaries and attribute explicitly (name the analyst/org) when a number can't be pinned to one primary source.
- Same-day events (an earnings call, an investor day) genuinely have zero outside coverage at write time — note "happened today, check next run" rather than searching repeatedly same-day. A direct official-domain query (org's own name + "press release") can beat generic search for a same-day primary figure.
- Dating-trap tells: a UK Budget story naming "Reeves" as Chancellor (the live timeline has Healey) is almost always Nov 2025 content bleeding through. More generally, check any "X happened/resolved" story against seen-items.md's internal timeline before trusting the article's own dateline.
