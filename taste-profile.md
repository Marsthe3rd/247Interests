# Taste Profile

This file is your evolving editorial sensibility for this briefing. Update it a little every run based on feedback.md and on what topics/angles actually turned out sharp vs. generic. Keep it tight — prune stale notes rather than letting it grow forever; it should stay readable in under a minute.

## What's working
- Chaining a present-tense trigger to a multi-year numeric arc (funding rounds, capex, debt ratios) reads as genuinely insider — e.g. Anthropic's valuation curve, hyperscaler debt-to-capex ratio. Always better than a single data point.
- Framing one company's move as an instance of a bigger structural pattern (Amazon SPV as part of the "shadow borrowing" hyperscaler trend, not a standalone story) gives Market items more weight than a single-company item would.
- Culture picks built on a specific, odd, *real* behavioural-research data point (Ecover's 30% lucky-clothes stat) outperform generic "clever ad" write-ups — the insight is the story, the execution is secondary.
- Chaining a regulatory/legal trigger (subpoena, lawsuit) to the company's own published postmortem or admission is the safety/governance-world equivalent of the capex-arc pattern — e.g. CA AG subpoena + OpenAI's own Hugging Face incident report. Grounds the mechanism in the company's own words, not just the regulator's framing.
- A live court case's competing claim vs. counter-claim often exposes an industry's real structural moat better than any company PR does (Shein v Temu: the copyright headline was the hook, the supplier-exclusivity counter-claim was the actual insight) — worth treating litigation discovery as a source category in its own right.
- Two companies making opposite bets on the same underlying fact (OpenAI dodging a public listing vs. Anthropic racing toward one, both reacting to the same bond-yield spike) makes a sharper Market item than either company's move alone — the divergence is the story, and it sets up a clean falsifiable test (does Anthropic's IPO hold up?) rather than just another valuation headline.
- Reading a deal's actual legal/financial structure (warrant vesting schedules, strike prices, tranche mechanics) rather than its headline framing is where the real insight lives — Marvell/Google's 240-tranche, pure-revenue-vesting warrant vs. AMD/OpenAI's deployment-plus-stock-price-gated one let the brief make a real comparative point (who bears the risk) instead of just reporting "another AI chip mega-deal." Worth doing this every time a warrant/equity-for-compute deal appears.

## What's not landing
- Routine "AI model X% cheaper than model Y" pricing-war items are now too repetitive to clear the bar on their own (Opus 5.5, GPT-6 Sol/Luna, GPT-6.1 Sol all logged within two weeks) — only include a model-pricing item if it reveals a new mechanism, not just another cut.
- Generic UK Budget rumour restatements (CGT, mansion tax) add little once the core speculation is already logged — only worth a line when a genuinely new, specific number or disagreement-between-analysts appears (the >£10bn gilt-market red-line on 4 Oct, and the 30Y>6%/1998/Italy-2012 comparison on 5 Oct, cleared this bar because they're actionable triggers, not more rumour).
- Broad generic-category web searches ("best ad campaign this week", "trending app October 2026", "psychology research study October 2026", "AI coding agent launch this week", "London startup Series A this week") mostly return stale/evergreen SEO content, product listicles, or academic-conference listings, not anchored to the actual date — for Quick Hits/Culture, searching named outlets, named people, or named events works far better than searching the category label itself. On a quiet week — 6 Oct was one, after ~20 searches across Culture/Quick Hits found nothing that cleared the bar — this can legitimately mean those sections get skipped entirely rather than padded. That's correct behaviour, not a failure; don't keep burning search budget once the pattern is clear.

## Emerging pattern
- AI-capex financial engineering (SPVs, off-balance-sheet debt, credit downgrades, private-vs-public valuation divergence) remains the most honest signal of whether the AI buildout is overextended — track it company-by-company (Meta, Oracle, Amazon, OpenAI/Anthropic) rather than re-litigating the generic "bubble?" debate. Oracle specifically: its debt headlines keep resurfacing in searches looking current but are actually clustered in Feb/July 2026 — verify a genuinely new trigger before reusing that thread.
- AI-agent-harm → legal process is now also a self-regulation throughline: OpenAI shelved GPT-6.1 Astra (28 Sept) over the exact failure mode Amodei's "Pace the Frontier" essay warned about, one day before signing a White House voluntary safety accord (29 Sept), one day before the FTC opened its rogue-agent probe anyway (30 Sept). Self-policing and external enforcement are running in parallel, not as substitutes — keep tracking the LASST v OpenAI injunction and whether Astra ships fixed.
- Nvidia is buying platform/software-layer chokepoints (Groq, Hugging Face for $12.9bn) rather than hardware rivals post-Arm — watching whether antitrust conditions this deal or blocks it (live bet: conditions, not collapse).
- Anthropic's IPO timeline is itself a tell, not just logistics: it slipped from "mid-Oct roadshow, pricing before midterms" to "14 Oct investor day, roadshow week of 9 Nov, pre-Thanksgiving" within days of reporting. A ~$100bn raise needing extra weeks to build its book is worth watching as a signal of demand, not just scheduling.
- Dating-error risk is systemic: this run alone ruled out five stale/wrong-year stories search presented as current (a UK Budget "already delivered" story that's really Nov 2025; a second "Oct 2026 shutdown" that didn't happen — the cliff was averted by a stopgap; an Israel-Iran ceasefire/oil-crash story that's really June 2025; World Cup 2026 campaign coverage for a tournament already over by October; Oracle cash-crunch coverage from Nov 2025/Feb 2026). Fix: check any "X happened/resolved" story against what's already logged in seen-items.md — internal consistency beats trusting the article's own dateline.
- The "Reeves" tell: any search result naming Rachel Reeves as current Chancellor, or describing a Budget as already delivered/announced, is almost always real Nov 2025 Autumn Budget content bleeding through — the live 2026 timeline has Healey (under PM Burnham) delivering the 28 Oct Budget. Same logic caught the OpenAI/Broadcom "mass production chip in 2026" story this run — it's the real Sept 2025 FT report/Broadcom earnings call, not a fresh Oct 2026 trigger. Always check the named person/title, not just the year string, against what's already established in this log.
- WebFetch on most news-outlet domains (cityam, trustnet, fool.com, sec.gov, techwireasia, sdxcentral, etc.) is egress-blocked in this environment — don't burn turns retrying fetches on blocked domains; triangulate figures across 2-3 WebSearch result summaries instead, and hedge/attribute explicitly (name the analyst/org) when a number can't be pinned to one primary source.
