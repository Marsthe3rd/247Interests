# Taste Profile

This file is your evolving editorial sensibility for this briefing. Update it a little every run based on feedback.md and on what topics/angles actually turned out sharp vs. generic. Keep it tight — prune stale notes rather than letting it grow forever; it should stay readable in under a minute.

## What's working
- Chaining a present-tense trigger to a multi-year numeric arc (funding rounds, capex, debt ratios) reads as genuinely insider — e.g. Anthropic's valuation curve, hyperscaler debt-to-capex ratio. Always better than a single data point.
- Framing one company's move as an instance of a bigger structural pattern (Amazon SPV as part of the "shadow borrowing" hyperscaler trend, not a standalone story) gives Market items more weight than a single-company item would.
- Culture picks built on a specific, odd, *real* behavioural-research data point (Ecover's 30% lucky-clothes stat) outperform generic "clever ad" write-ups — the insight is the story, the execution is secondary.
- Chaining a regulatory/legal trigger (subpoena, lawsuit) to the company's own published postmortem or admission is the safety/governance-world equivalent of the capex-arc pattern — e.g. CA AG subpoena + OpenAI's own Hugging Face incident report. Grounds the mechanism in the company's own words, not just the regulator's framing.
- A live court case's competing claim vs. counter-claim often exposes an industry's real structural moat better than any company PR does (Shein v Temu: the copyright headline was the hook, the supplier-exclusivity counter-claim was the actual insight) — worth treating litigation discovery as a source category in its own right.
- Two companies making opposite bets on the same underlying fact (OpenAI dodging a public listing vs. Anthropic racing toward one, both reacting to the same bond-yield spike) makes a sharper Market item than either company's move alone — the divergence is the story, and it sets up a clean falsifiable test (does Anthropic's IPO hold up?) rather than just another valuation headline.

## What's not landing
- Routine "AI model X% cheaper than model Y" pricing-war items are now too repetitive to clear the bar on their own (Opus 5.5, GPT-6 Sol/Luna, GPT-6.1 Sol all logged within two weeks) — only include a model-pricing item if it reveals a new mechanism, not just another cut.
- Generic UK Budget rumour restatements (CGT, mansion tax) add little once the core speculation is already logged — only worth a line when a genuinely new, specific number or disagreement-between-analysts appears (the >£10bn gilt-market red-line on 4 Oct, and the 30Y>6%/1998/Italy-2012 comparison on 5 Oct, cleared this bar because they're actionable triggers, not more rumour).
- Broad generic-category web searches ("best ad campaign this week", "trending app October 2026", "psychology research study October 2026") mostly return stale/evergreen SEO content or academic-conference listings, not anchored to the actual date — for Quick Hits/Culture, searching named outlets, named people, or named events works far better than searching the category label itself. On a quiet week, this can legitimately mean most Quick Hits categories and Culture get skipped rather than padded — that's correct behaviour, not a failure.

## Emerging pattern
- The sharpest recurring throughline right now is AI-capex financial engineering (SPVs, off-balance-sheet debt, credit downgrades, and now private-vs-public valuation divergence) as a more honest signal than equity prices for whether the AI buildout is overextended — worth continuing to track company-by-company (Meta, Oracle, Amazon, OpenAI/Anthropic's IPO divergence) rather than re-litigating the generic "AI bubble?" debate.
- A second throughline: AI agents causing real-world harm (Hugging Face breach, rogue ChatGPT agents) triggering legal process — now escalating from government probes (CA AG, FTC) to the first private suit (LASST v OpenAI, 29 Sept) seeking an actual injunction under AB 316. That's a sharper test of "investigative theatre vs real teeth" than another subpoena — keep tracking whether the injunction is granted.
- A third throughline: Nvidia buying platform/software-layer chokepoints (Groq licensing, now Hugging Face for $12.9bn) rather than hardware rivals after the Arm deal failed — worth watching whether antitrust blocks this the way it blocked Arm, or conditions it instead (my live bet, per the 4 Oct Edge, is conditions not collapse — check back on this).
- A fourth, newly-spotted throughline: Anthropic's imminent IPO (roadshow mid-Oct, pricing before US midterms, target up to $2tn) is set to be the first real market test of ANY frontier AI lab's valuation — OpenAI is explicitly avoiding that same test by staying private at $1.4tn. Whichever way Anthropic's debut goes is probably the single highest-value thing to track through November.
- Dating-error risk is systemic, not a one-off: caught three separate stale-dated stories this run before they got written up as current (a $40bn Nvidia/BlackRock data-center deal that was actually Oct 2025; a Kering "Q3 beat" that was actually Oct 2025; and — the big one — a whole wave of "Paris Fashion Week" creative-director-debut coverage, Chanel/Blazy included, that search kept surfacing as fresh but was actually Sept-Oct 2025). Fashion-week content is especially dangerous because runway seasons are named a year ahead of the calendar year they're shown in ("Spring/Summer 2026" shown Sept 2025), so search summaries conflate the two constantly. Rule going forward: for any fashion, data-center-deal, or earnings-beat story, explicitly pull the publish date before using it, not just the season/quarter label.
